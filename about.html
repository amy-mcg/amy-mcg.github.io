About Me

Bio

I come from a big ol' Irish family. My home is small village called Kilcar in rural Donegal, but I lived much of my early life on the sunny south coast of England. I moved away at 18 to go to university, and have been moderately nomadic ever since.

When I'm not in the lab or in the field I am usually swimming. As I am a cisgender white woman in my thirties I think wild swimming is an acceptable substitute for a personality, and there is nothing that brings me more joy than throwing myself into a lake or the sea. If you would like to read more about my aquatic adventures I have a rarely updated swimming blog, or you can listen to me waffle on about mountains on my friend Ronni's podcast.

Alternatively you may find me at St. Mary's as, having had a season ticket from the age of 7, I for some reason remain a Southampton FC fan and I suspect it may be too late to change my allegiances.

My other interests include watching cricket, and listening to cricket, and badly playing cricket. During the COVID pandemic I got a shout out on TMS and it is still one of my favourite things to have ever happened.

Although I claim to only like sad indie music I'm actually fairly eclectic in my tastes - sometimes I even listen to happy indie music. You can get far too much information about my past, current and, eventually, future music taste by digging into my last.fm account.

As someone who studies climate change, it is impossible to be apolitical. My politics, however, are not exclusively environmental. First and foremost, I am a socialist, and believe the ongoing climate crisis is a product of our fundamentally flawed economic system which continuously places profit before people and planet. If you would like to argue with me you can find me on bluesky, or if you just want to shout into the void you can tell me off on the website formerly known as twitter.

Finally, I am always happy to be contacted should you wish to discuss science/music/cricket/Southampton Football Club, and especially if you'd like to tell me how amazing I'm doing. I can be reached by email here. I am autistic, and sometimes get overwhelmed, so please don't be offended if I don't get back to you straight away.

Talks

I absolutely love sharing my research with interested audiences of all shapes and sizes. I regularly give talks for geographical and geological societies, natural history groups, and community organisations. One talk I am particularly excited to be giving at the moment explores Bavelonia, an ancient land bridge that once connected Britain to continental Europe:

Britain the Peninsula: Lost Landscapes beneath the North Sea

Britain was not always an island. More than a million years ago, a vast lowland landscape connected Britain to continental Europe across what is now the North Sea. In this talk, I will introduce Bavelonia, a land bridge that persisted for hundreds of thousands of years, experienced dramatic environmental change, and witnessed the arrival of some of the earliest humans in northern Europe. Using evidence from the seabed, including seismic surveys and sediment cores, I will reveal what this lost landscape looked like and reflect on why it matters.

If you would like me to come and speak, I am best contacted by email.
